**Math as joy, not a chore.** 
What happens when you treat arithmetic as a creative playground? These six core design principles guide every level, feature, and interaction within CalcQuest, ensuring an experience that respects the player’s intelligence and time. 

## Principle 1: Math as Puzzle, Not Drill

CalcQuest treats arithmetic as a creative problem-solving activity rather than repetitive practice. Players engage with numbers through strategic thinking, not speed or memorization.

### Application

- Puzzles have multiple valid approaches, rewarding experimentation.
- Time pressure is minimized in campaign mode.
- Emphasis on "how do I get there?" rather than "how fast can I recall?"

## Principle 2: Accessibility First

The game should be approachable for players of all ages and skill levels while still offering depth for those who seek it.

### Application

- Core mechanics require only basic arithmetic knowledge.
- Early levels teach concepts through play.
- Difficulty is layered, not gated by prior expertise.

## Principle 3: Respect Player Time

Players should feel their time is valued. Sessions can be short without sacrificing satisfaction.

### Application

- Levels are designed to be completable in a few minutes.
- Progress saves automatically.
- No mandatory waiting or cooldown mechanics.

## Principle 4: Player Agency

Players should have meaningful choices and control over their experience.

### Application

- Multiple game modes cater to different preferences.
- Custom level creation empowers creativity.
- No forced tutorials for returning players.

## Principle 5: Transparent Experience

The game should not obscure mechanics or manipulate players.

### Application

- Rules are clearly communicated within levels.
- No hidden costs or surprise monetization.
- Honest difficulty representation.

## Principle 6: Community-Driven Longevity

Content created by players extends the game beyond developer-made levels.

### Application

- Level editor is a core feature, not an afterthought.
- Sharing mechanisms (QR codes, share codes) are simple to use.
- Community content is treated with the same respect as official content.
